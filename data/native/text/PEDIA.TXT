@PEDIAGOVT
@width=600
@title=Civilization: Governments
The government you choose decides a great deal about your
civilization. It sets how each city's trade is divided
between taxes, science and luxuries, how many units and
improvements your cities can build and keep up, and how
much say your citizens have in the affairs of state.
^
Despotism and Monarchy, along with Communism and
Fundamentalism later on, put power firmly in the ruler's
hands. They suit rapid expansion, large standing armies and
leaders who like to keep total control of their people.
^
The Republic and Democracy give power to the citizens.
Free citizens produce a great deal of trade, so your
treasury and your research grow quickly. The price
is unhappiness whenever military units leave their
cities, and a Senate that may interfere with your
foreign policy, sometimes at the worst possible moment.
^
To change your government, start a Revolution by choosing
Revolution from the Kingdom menu. A short period of
Anarchy follows before the new government takes power.

@PEDIAGOVT0
@width=600
@title=Civilization Government: Anarchy
Anarchy is not really a form of government at all,
but the lack of one. It sets in when a government
collapses under civil disorder, or when you start
a Revolution. Once some turns of chaos have
passed, a new government can take over.
^
Anarchy works like Despotism (see that page),
except that corruption is extremely high.
While it lasts, your civilization collects
no taxes and makes no progress in research.

@PEDIAGOVT1
@width=600
@title=Civilization Government: Despotism
^Unit Support
Each unit beyond the city's size in number costs 1 shield per turn.
Settlers eat 1 food per turn.
^
^Happiness
Martial law: each military unit in
a city, up to a maximum of three,
makes one unhappy citizen content.
^
^Corruption and Waste
Corruption and waste are high.
How much a city loses depends on
how far it lies from your capital.
^
^Special
No rate for science, taxes or luxuries can be
set above 60%. In addition, any square that would
produce 3 or more food, shields or trade produces
1 less. (Cities with a Palace are no exception.)
^
^Hints
Because of its heavy corruption, Despotism
is almost always a poor choice. Switch to
Monarchy as soon as you have the chance.


@PEDIAGOVT2
@width=600
@title=Civilization Government: Monarchy
^Unit Support
Each city supports 3 units for free; each extra unit costs 1 shield.
Settlers eat 1 food per turn.
^
^Happiness
Martial law: each military unit in
a city, up to a maximum of three,
makes one unhappy citizen content.
^
^Corruption and Waste
Corruption and waste are moderate. They
grow larger the farther a city lies from
your capital, so distant cities suffer most.
^
^Special
The rate for science, taxes or
luxuries can be set no higher than 70%.
^
^Hints
Monarchy is an excellent choice
for a young civilization.


@PEDIAGOVT3
@width=600
@title=Civilization Government: Communism
^Unit Support
Each city supports 3 units for free; each extra unit costs 1 shield.
Settlers eat 1 food per turn.
^
^Happiness
Martial law: each city can use as many
as three military units to keep order,
and each of them makes two unhappy
citizens content, for a maximum of six.
^
^Corruption and Waste
With the state running the whole economy,
there is no room left for organised
crime. Cities suffer no corruption at all.
^
^Special
Every Spy you build starts as a
veteran. The rate for science, taxes or
luxuries can be set no higher than 80%.
^
^Hints
Communism suits large, sprawling empires with
big armies to support. Send your veteran Spies to
borrow advances from the capitalists next door.

@PEDIAGOVT4
@width=600
@title=Civilization Government: Fundamentalism
^Unit Support
Each city supports up to 10 units for
free; each unit beyond that costs 1 shield.
Settlers eat 2 food per turn. Fanatics
can only be built under this government.
^
^Happiness
Nobody is ever unhappy. Not one citizen. Ever.
^
^Corruption and Waste
Corruption is low. On the other
hand, science output is cut in half,
since faith comes before inquiry.
^
^Special
Improvements that would normally make unhappy
citizens content, such as Temples, instead
collect tithes: each one brings in one gold for
every citizen it would have calmed, and none of
them needs upkeep. Acts of terrorism by your
Spies and Diplomats cause less diplomatic
outrage than usual. The rate for science, taxes
or luxuries can be set no higher than 80%.
^
^Hints
You will never face unrest and your income will be strong,
but expect your research to grind almost to a halt.


@PEDIAGOVT5
@width=600
@title=Civilization Government: Republic
^Unit Support
Every unit costs 1 shield per
turn. Settlers eat 2 food per turn.
^
^Happiness
Each military unit beyond the first that is away from friendly
territory upsets a citizen back home. Units inside a city, or
in a fortress up to 3 squares from one, cause no such trouble.
^
^Corruption and Waste
Corruption and waste are low, but they
still grow the farther a city lies from
your capital, so distant cities lose more.
^
^Special
Every square that already produces
at least 1 trade produces 1 more. The
rate for science, taxes or luxuries
can be set no higher than 80%.
^
^Hints
The Republic gives your science and tax income
a big boost, though you will probably need to
spend some of your trade on luxuries to keep
people content. Armies serving abroad become
harder and more expensive to maintain. The
Women's Suffrage wonder can ease the burden.

@PEDIAGOVT6
@width=600
@title=Civilization Government: Democracy
^Unit Support
Every unit costs 1 shield per
turn. Settlers eat 2 food per turn.
^
^Happiness
Each unit away from friendly territory leaves two
citizens back home unhappy. Units inside a city, or in a
fortress up to 3 squares from one, cause no such trouble.
^
^Corruption and Waste
No corruption and no waste whatsoever.
^
^Special
Every square that already produces
at least 1 trade produces 1 more.
Neither your units nor your cities
can ever be bribed, by any means.
There is no upper limit on the rates
for science, taxes or luxuries.
^
^Hints
Democracy brings enormous income and rapid
research. However, the heavy unhappiness caused
by units in the field makes it practical mainly
for large, advanced civilizations that can afford
to keep the peace. A higher luxury rate and the
right Wonders will help keep your voters content.
