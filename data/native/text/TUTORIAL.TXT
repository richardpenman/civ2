@FIRSTUNIT2
@width=500
@title=Civ Tutorial: Units
Fortify units inside your cities to guard
them against barbarians and enemies. Phalanx
and Pikemen are the best defenders, but
Warriors, Archers and Legion help too.
^
Settlers found new cities, and also
build roads, mines and irrigation
around them. Try to keep at least
one Settlers unit busy at all times.
^
Fast units such as Horsemen are
good for scouting unknown lands.
^
For attacking, use Archers, Chariot,
Elephant and Catapult units.

@CITYSTUFF
@width=420
@x=180
@title=Civ Tutorial: City Resources
What your citizens produce depends on
the terrain around the city. The city
screen shows each square being worked,
with symbols for the food (wheat),
shields and trade (arrows) it yields.
^
Click on those squares to move your workers around.
Different squares yield different amounts of each
resource, and Settlers can improve most of them
with roads, irrigation or mines. Try out different
arrangements until you find the one you like best.
^
You can also take citizens off the map entirely
and make them Entertainers. They provide
luxuries, but no food or anything else.

@HELP1
@width=500
@title=Civ Tutorial: Basic Strategy #1
Keep at least one strong defender
in every city, and preferably two.
^
Strike a balance between
improving the cities you
have and founding new ones.
^
Do not expand faster than you can
defend. Barbarians and enemies
can appear without warning.

@HELP2
@width=500
@title=Civ Tutorial: Basic Strategy #2
Stay at peace with neighbours stronger than you.
Cut off weaker neighbours and conquer them.
^
Triremes and other ships can
explore the oceans and carry
your units to other continents.
^
Caravans set up trade routes
that boost your economy. Trade
brings in both gold and knowledge.
^
As your civilization grows,
send Diplomats to find out
what your rivals are planning.

@DISORDER
@width=480
@title=Civ Tutorial: Civil Disorder
@button=More
Civil Disorder breaks out
when a city's unhappy citizens
outnumber its happy ones. While
it lasts, the city neither
grows nor produces anything.
^
To restore order, you can:
^* Spend a bigger share of trade on Luxuries.
^* Station military units in the city.
^* Build a Temple, Cathedral or Colosseum.
^* Turn some citizens into entertainers.
^* Change your government to Fundamentalism.
^
Press 'Details' for more
help, or if you are stuck.

@DISORDER2
@width=600
@title=Civ Tutorial: Civil Disorder, More
1) Luxuries: Choose 'Tax Rate' from the KINGDOM
menu and raise the share of trade spent on
luxuries. A rate of 20% often works well,
especially under Monarchy or the Republic. Every
2 luxuries in a city make one citizen happy.
^
2) Martial Law: Under Despotism,
Monarchy or Communism, military units
stationed in a city help keep order.
Each unit, up to a maximum of 3 per
city, makes one unhappy citizen content.
^
3) Temples, Colosseums and Cathedrals:
Each of these makes at least one unhappy
citizen content. Remember that a city in
disorder produces nothing, so you may have
to 'Buy' the building outright, or use
entertainers for a while until it is finished.

@DISORDER3
@width=600
@title=Civ Tutorial: Civil Disorder, More
4) Entertainers: On the city screen, click
a square being worked in the city's area to
take its worker off the land. The citizen
becomes an entertainer, who makes no food,
shields or trade, but gives 2 luxuries.
^
5) Fundamentalism: Once you know the advance
of Fundamentalism, you can adopt it as your
government. Under Fundamentalism nobody is ever
unhappy, although your science will suffer.

@SHIPS
@width=480
@y=5
@title=Civ Tutorial: Ships
Your first ship is ready! Ships
can explore the oceans and carry
your units to other continents.
^
Be careful: a Trireme is in danger of sinking
unless it ends each turn next to land.
^
To load a unit, put it to sleep (press 's') while
the ship is in port, then sail the ship away.
If the ship is already offshore, simply move the
unit onto it from a neighbouring land square.
^
To unload your units, press
'u' or simply click the ship.

@RAILROADS
@width=480
@title=Civ Tutorial: Railroads
You have learned Railroad, a great
step forward! To build a railroad, move
Settlers or Engineers onto a square
that already has a road and press 'r'.
^
Units can travel great distances along railroads.
Railroads also increase the shields produced
by squares that already yield 2 or more.

@FARMLAND
@width=480
@title=Civ Tutorial: Farmland
You have learned Refrigeration, which
lets your people preserve food far better
than before. To create farmland, move
Settlers or Engineers onto a square that is
already irrigated and press 'i'. Farmland
gives a nearby city extra food, but only
if that city has built a Supermarket.

@AIRUNIT
@width=480
@y=5
@title=Civ Tutorial: Air Units
Your first air unit is ready! Aircraft
can bomb enemy units and cities,
defend your territory against enemy
bombers, and scout out distant lands.
^
Watch the fuel: an air unit must return to an
airbase or a friendly city before it runs out.

@REPUBLIC
@width=480
@title=Civ Tutorial: Republic
A new form of government, the REPUBLIC, is
now available to you. Changing your government
can bring great benefits, but it can also
bring serious problems, so plan carefully
before you make the switch. The 'Governments'
entries in the Civilopedia explain every
type in detail. When you are ready to change,
choose Revolution from the Kingdom menu.

@DEMOCRATS
@width=600
@title=Civ Tutorial: Republic & Democracy
You now rule as a %STRING0. This is a big change
from the governments that came before, and it
brings new challenges as well as new rewards.
^
Free citizens produce much more trade,
but they are also harder to keep happy.
The best remedy is to spend some of
that trade on luxuries: open 'Tax Rate'
in the Kingdom menu and raise Luxuries
to 20%%. The extra trade from your free
citizens makes this easy to afford.
^
Military units outside your cities now make
citizens unhappy, so you may need to bring some
of your units home, or even disband a few of
them. It can be painful, but the gains in science
and gold are usually well worth the trouble.

@TRADE
@width=420
@title=Civ Tutorial: Trade
You have learned Trade! Your cities can now build
CARAVAN units to carry goods to distant lands.
Caravans quickly boost both your gold and your
science, especially those sent from large cities.

@CARAVAN
@width=440
@y=5
@title=Civ Tutorial: Caravans
Your first CARAVAN is ready! Send it off to some
faraway city and open a trade route there. Routes
to small, nearby cities of your own earn less
than routes to large, faraway cities of a rival.
It also pays to find a city that wants the goods
you carry; clicking on a rival's city shows what
it demands. Caravans can help build Wonders, too.

@WRITING
@width=420
@title=Civ Tutorial: Writing
You have learned Writing! Your cities can
now build DIPLOMAT units and reach out to
other civilizations. A Diplomat can set up
an embassy, keep an eye on your allies,
and even steal advances from your rivals.

@SEAFARING
@width=420
@title=Civ Tutorial: Seafaring
You have learned Seafaring! You can now
build EXPLORER units to discover new lands.
Explorers move 3 squares a turn whatever
the terrain, so they cover ground very
quickly. You may want to build TRIREME
ships to carry them, and your other units,
across the sea to faraway continents.

@DAMAGE
@width=520
@title=Civ Tutorial: Damaged Units
This unit has been damaged, as shown by
the coloured bar on top of its shield.
Green means it is mostly healthy; yellow,
and then red, mean more serious damage.
^
If the unit skips a whole turn (press the SPACE bar),
it recovers some of its strength. Units heal faster
in cities and fortresses. Barracks restore a ground
unit to full health in one turn, and a Port Facility
or Airport does the same for ships and air units.
