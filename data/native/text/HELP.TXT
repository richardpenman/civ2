@ADVANCES
@width=600
@title=Advances Editor Help
^AI VALUE sets how highly computer players rate
^this advance when choosing what to research.
^
^MODIFIER is added to or subtracted from the AI Value.
^Civilized tribes favor positive modifiers.
^Militaristic tribes favor negative modifiers.
^We advise keeping both values between -5 and 5.
^
^EPOCH and CATEGORY together choose the icon shown here.
^Many advances, or even all of them,
^may share the same icon. That is fine.
^
^To remove an advance from the game, set both of its
^prerequisites to NO. NIL means no prerequisite is needed;
^an advance with NIL for both is known from the start.
^
^Warning: FUTURE TECHNOLOGY must stay linked to the
^tree. If any tribe cannot reach it, the game will crash.

@TERRAIN
@width=600
@title=Terrain Editor Help
^A terrain's DEFENSE VALUE raises the defense
^of the units standing on it, as follows:
^
^    Defense = Unit Defense x 50%% x Terrain Defense
^
^Thus a Phalanx standing in Forest defends at 3:
^    2 x 3 x 1/2 = 3
^
^No matter how high a terrain's MOVE COST, every unit can still
^cover at least one square per turn.
^
^GOVERNMENT FOR AI is the lowest government at which
^computer tribes will carry out that terrain order.
^ 
^To edit the ocean graphics at zoom levels that draw coastlines, 
use Coasts 1 to 4 for that. At the closer 
zoom levels, where no coastlines are drawn, 
edit the ordinary ocean icon instead.


@UNITS
@width=600
@title=Units Editor Help
^BUILD COST is shown in tens of shields: the real
^cost is the value shown x10. Never set it above 18.
^
^HOLDS ON SHIP is the most units this ship can carry.
^
^RANGE is how many turns an air unit can stay aloft.
^It must end its final turn at a base, or it will crash.
^
^ATTACK FACTOR sets the odds of scoring a hit when attacking. 
^An attack value of 99 makes a unit nuclear, with many side effects.
^
^DEFENSE FACTOR sets the odds of scoring a hit when defending.
^
^HIT POINTS is the damage (x10) the unit can take before it is destroyed.
^
^FIREPOWER is the damage dealt with each successful hit.
^
^A battle always ends with one side destroyed, so both units may
^take many hits along the way. Combat stops as soon as either unit's
^damage reaches its hit points, and that unit is the one destroyed.


@TRIBES
@width=600
@title=Tribe Editor Help^
^The CITY STYLE chosen here only affects tribes run by the computer. 
^Human tribes use the style their player picked, or else
^the standard ancient style of the Bronze and Stone Ages.
^You can edit a tribe's NAME, its LEADER names and its
^leader's attitudes. You cannot edit its COLOR. Each
^color always belongs to 3 tribes, but only one of them
^is active in a scenario at a time. Either of the other
^two may take its place if the active tribe is wiped out.
^Every tribe takes one of 3 ATTACK attitudes:
^  Aggressive: quick to start a fight.
^  Rational: prefers to negotiate.
^  Neutral: somewhere in between.
^Every tribe takes one of 3 EXPAND attitudes:
^  Expansionist: sends Settlers far and wide and tries to seize cities.
^  Perfectionist: wants a few rich cities rather than many average ones.
^  Normal: somewhere in between.
^Every tribe takes one of 3 CIVILIZE attitudes:
^  Civilized: prefers peaceful advances.
^  Militaristic: prefers military advances.
^  Normal: somewhere in between.


@EFFECTS
@width=600
@title=Universal Effects Editor Help
^IMPORTANT: Changes to these values do not take effect
^right away. First save the game or the scenario,
^then load it again. Before you do, the figure in parentheses shows
^the new value you set, and the other one shows the value in force.
^
^Most effects explain themselves, but a few need a word or two:
^
^CITY SIZE (default 7) is the size at which one citizen
^becomes unhappy at Chieftain level. Each higher difficulty
^level lowers it by 1, from 6 at Warlord down to 2 at
^Deity. Changing it shifts the value for every level.
^
^RIOT FACTOR is how many cities you can own before 
^your empire's sheer size starts to cause unhappiness.
^
^It is better to change the TECH PARADIGM from the Cheat menu than here.
^
^TRANSFORM TERRAIN is twice the number of turns
^an Engineer needs to finish a transformation.

@HELPUNITKILLED
@width=600
@title=Events Editor Help
^UNITKILLED
^
^This event fires when all three conditions are true:
^  1. A unit of the named type is killed in battle.
^  2. The attacker belongs to the named tribe.
^  3. The defender belongs to the named tribe.
^
^Either tribe name, or both, may be replaced with
^'Anybody' so that the event applies to every tribe.


@HELPNEGOTIATION
@width=600
@title=Events Editor Help
^NEGOTIATION
^
^Stops the 'talker' tribe from talking with the 'listener' tribe.
^You can limit it to human tribes or to computer tribes;
^choosing 'humanorcomputer' sets no limit at all.
^
^To block talks in both directions, add a second
^event with the talker and listener swapped.
^
^IMPORTANT: this trigger carries a hidden action of its 
^own, which also blocks all talks between the two
^tribes. That is handy in historical scenarios where
^two governments never met. Be careful, though: tribes
^try to negotiate very often, especially the computer
^tribes, so if you attach a text popup to this event it
^may appear so often that the game becomes unplayable.


@HELPCITYTAKEN
@width=600
@title=Events Editor Help
^CITYTAKEN
^
^This event fires when all three conditions are true:
^  1. The named city changes hands.
^  2. The attacker belongs to the named tribe.
^  3. The defender belongs to the named tribe.
^
^Either tribe name, or both, may be replaced with
^'Anybody' so that the event applies to every tribe.
^
^This is very useful for reacting to the fall of important cities.
^For example, trigger on the loss of a capital and
^use MOVEUNIT to send units within a map rectangle
^around it back toward the capital to retake it.


@HELPTURN
@width=600
@title=Events Editor Help
^TURN
^
^This event fires at the start of the chosen turn.
^Choose 'Every' to make it fire every turn. Keep in mind
^that turn numbers and game years differ; the 'Set Game
^Year' item in the Cheat menu shows the current turn
^number. Useful for timed reinforcements in historical
^scenarios, for replaying troop movements, and much more.


@HELPTURNINTERVAL
@width=600
@title=Events Editor Help
^TURNINTERVAL
^
^A repeating trigger. The number is the gap, in
^turns, between firings: 'turn=4', for example,
^fires every fourth turn. Handy for pushing units
^toward particular objectives again and again.


@HELPRANDOMTURN
@width=600
@title=Events Editor Help
^RANDOMTURN
^
^This event fires at random. Each turn, the game divides a
^random number by the denominator you entered, and the
^event fires when the remainder is 0. In other words, the
^odds are 1 in X on any given turn, with X being the
^number you entered: a denominator of 30, for example,
^gives a 1 in 30 chance per turn. Combine it with JUSTONCE
^for a random event that happens only a single time.

@HELPCREATEUNIT
@width=600
@title=Events Editor Help
^CREATEUNIT
^
^Creates a new unit at no cost. The game first tries to place
^it at the first location in the list; if that fails, it tries
^each of the following locations in turn. You need several
^locations because a spot may be occupied by an enemy unit,
^or may simply be invalid, such as a land unit on ocean.
^
^Triggerattacker is the tribe that set off the event as
^the attacker: the winner of the fight in a UNITKILLED
^event, or the captor of the city in a CITYTAKEN event.
^
^Triggerdefender is its opposite number: the
^tribe whose unit or city came under attack.
^
^Triggerreceiver means the tribe that acquired the advance in a
^RECEIVEDTECHNOLOGY event. It can only be used with that trigger.

@HELPPLAYWAVEFILE
@width=600
@title=Events Editor Help
^PLAYWAVEFILE
^
^Plays a .wav sound of your choice. Type its exact file
^name. The game looks for it first in the scenario's
^own sound folder, then in the game's main sound folder.
^No other folders are searched, to avoid clashes between files

@HELPMOVEUNIT

@title=Events Editor Help
^MOVEUNIT
^Orders a number of units, up to the count you state, of a given type 
^and belonging to a given tribe, inside a map rectangle, to head for a target 
^location. Use ANYUNIT when the type of unit does not matter.
^Only units that are not fortified,
^asleep or on sentry duty, that have no
^goto order already, are not building a
^fortress and are not nuclear will obey.

^
^IMPORTANT: the corners of a 'maprect' must be listed in a fixed order.
^Corner 1 is the upper left, corner 2 the upper right, corner 3
^the lower right and corner 4 the lower left, as in this sketch:
^
^                                     1----2
^                                     |    |
^                                     4----3

@HELPFOREST
@width=600
@title=Forest Help
^Forest, Hills and Mountains each have 16 different
^images, so that ranges of them look continuous across
^the map. Which image a tile uses depends only on
^which of its neighbouring tiles have the same terrain.
^The table below lists all 16 combinations, each direction set followed
^by its image number. For example, image 2 is used only when the tile's
^one Forest neighbour lies to the NE. Forest neighbours to the NW and SE
^give image 11, and Forest on all four diagonal sides gives image 16. In
^general, add 1 for NE, 2 for SE, 4 for SW and 8 for NW, then add 1 more.
^
^  None = 1         NE = 2           SE = 3           NE&SE = 4
^  SW = 5           NE&SW = 6        SE&SW = 7        NE,SE,SW = 8
^  NW = 9           NE&NW = 10       SE&NW = 11       NE,SE,NW = 12
^ SW&NW = 13 NE,SW,NW = 14 SE,SW,NW = 15 NE,SE,SW,NW = 16
^
^The background image is the base layer drawn underneath Forest, Hills or Mountains.

@HELPRIVER
@width=600
@title=River Help
^River images come in 16 variants, so that waterways join up smoothly from 
^one tile to the next. Which variant a tile uses depends only on which of 
^its neighbouring tiles are also River; the terrain of the other neighbours 
^makes no difference. The table below lists all 16 combinations, each 
^direction set followed by its image number. For example, image 2 is used
^only when the tile's one River neighbour lies to the NE. River neighbours
^to the NW and SE give image 11, and River on all four diagonal sides gives
^image 16. To work out the image for any tile, add 1 for a River neighbour
^to the NE, 2 for SE, 4 for SW and 8 for NW, then add 1 more to the total.
^
^  None = 1         NE = 2           SE = 3           NE&SE = 4
^  SW = 5           NE&SW = 6        SE&SW = 7        NE,SE,SW = 8
^  NW = 9           NE&NW = 10       SE&NW = 11       NE,SE,NW = 12
^ SW&NW = 13 NE,SW,NW = 14 SE,SW,NW = 15 NE,SE,SW,NW = 16
^
^There is one river mouth image for each direction, 4 in all:
^  NE, SE, SW, NW (clockwise)


@HELPCOAST
@width=600
@title=Coast Help
^Many coast images are needed so that a landmass of any shape gets
^a seamless shoreline. You are free to change their colours and
^what they show, but every image must keep some kind of shoreline,
^and the points where the shore meets the edges of the tile must
^stay exactly where they are. That is what makes the coasts join
^up without seams from one tile to the next. For a consistent look,
^if you make a major change to the colours or the composition,
^apply it to all of the coast images rather than just some of
^them, or the odd ones out will stand out clearly on the map. 
^
^To change the ocean at zoom levels that show coastlines, 
^edit Coast 1. To change it at the closer zoom levels, where 
^coastlines are not drawn, edit the ocean icon instead.
